2016_March 22

mother tongue is the one you count in

I have a microwave that’s also an oven.
its fake versatility already makes me resent it.
but once I craved gratin
rushed downstairs
to where they sell the store brand cooking cream
and a pair of scissors
to cut the corner that should have been there
at the top of the carton.

I found only a red plastic cap
climbed back up
peeled, cut, stirred, poured, sliced
googled, heated, thought I got it
enticed, pressed, turned
waited.
the gratin emerged.

5:30 p.m. and I missed
the first sunny day of spring
and a call —
a foreign country code

probably a troll

but the number wouldn’t let me go.

two three two three four o.

there were days when I recited this number by heart.

my glasses lie next to me
mocking the memory I’ve lost
to the weight of the present.

if I had woken up in the present
maybe I could have calmed a terrified elder
answered a wary TV reporter
in a language that once was mine
listened to the silence of a dead line
whispering hello?
fading into the echo of that h
that +33 never summon me to utter

mumbled an old phrase:
sorry, wrong number.

I type in the username and password
I hope I once chose for my skype account
ten dollars and three cents
virtual money in a currency I’ve never earned
to use it — fees grow

two three two three four o.
a number I know.

Hello?

Hello.

a middle-aged woman
a clerk
maybe at an embassy
or just a mistake
how does a mistake drift free
from elsewhere to +33?

Yes?
I got a call from this number.
I didn’t call anyone.
Two hours ago. Who am I speaking with?
No, I didn’t call.

[and why insist? it’s just a foreign number and you’re burning US dollars chasing a blunder.]

My name is Rony, I live in France.
Someone from this number called me.

Rony?

Mom?

I didn’t recognize your voice.

Neither did I.

I must have pressed it by accident.

I thought something happened.

No, all good.

Yeah. All good.

Well, this is costing me a lot.

Okay. Bye.

this time, not the store brand
but the pricey organic stand
yeast and a fancy chocolate bar
Japanese vanilla and buckwheat flour in a Mason jar
eggs and butter laced with French sea salt
went up, wiped my face, sought
crushed, spilled, stirred, improved
balanced, improvised, removed
sat down.
waited.
recognized —

the scent of a simple chocolate cake
spreading from the microwave-oven
rising through electric heat like foam
wandering like the memory of numbers
playing tricks like home.

One post, many cuts. A post is written once as a list of parts: text, image or clip. The page shows it two ways: read, as an essay with the clips set into it, and swipe, panel by panel for a phone.

Two languages, one stream. Every piece appears in the language it was written in. A switch shows the other language, translated automatically, and the translated page always says so and links back to the original.

Socials come after. Under every post there is a kit for taking it out: a carousel made from your highlights, a short clip for a reel, a LinkedIn text, each with a caption that links back here. The full version only exists on the blog.

Weight and access. Opening a post loads about 60 to 200 KB of images. Clips are 0.3 to 2 MB each and load only on play. Every clip has captions and a written transcript, made by machine for this draft, so check them before publishing.
